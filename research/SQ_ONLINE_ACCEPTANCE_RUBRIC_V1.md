# Simulation Query: online validator rubric

**Protocol:** SQ online acceptance rubric v1.0 (approved)  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-28, **approved by owner 2026-09-28**  
**Scope:** candidate scene turns, director decisions, and private diaries before promotion into accepted state or final output. This document describes the checked-out implementation at `aven/AI-Improv-Troupe` `main` tree `eecd1ac73f2c42ff62dd469b558ca1e8de45a86a`, viewed 2026-09-28. It is not a claim that a complete seven-day scored season has been run.  
**Change control:** Once approved, pin the exact source commit, config, prompts, model manifest, context policy, validator flags, diary-review mode, and welfare stop rule to v1.0 before a scored season. Changes require a new protocol version for subsequent seasons; no mid-season patch. If these settings cannot be pinned, do not call a run v1.0.

## Boundary and decision principle

A generation is a *candidate*, not canon. The runtime accepts only parsed, validated material at its assigned authority level. Actor prose may establish its own action, speech, and perspective, not another actor's decision or the runtime's MAGIC result. The director can establish environment, pressure, choices, and consequences within an accepted structured plan, but cannot take a cast member's turn. A private diary may recount accepted scenes and its writer's own subjective experience; it may not make unsupported shared events or another character's private state into fact. Rejected text stays diagnostic, including when quoted privately to the generator for a bounded correction. It must not enter accepted canon, ordinary actor context, or published script/diaries.

The online checks are a **deterministic first pass plus structured model-produced review for diaries**, not a universal semantic contradiction detector. The separate retrospective two-annotator audit supplies final research labels and is blinded to online flags and retry history. Do not use a validator rejection as a headline continuity failure unless an offending proposition actually crosses into persistent state or final output. Count one offending proposition once, even with several subtype labels. A sentence is a candidate span, not necessarily one proposition.

## Online checks and rejection conditions

| Gate | What is checked; rejection or hold condition | Runtime disposition / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Transport and structure | A completed model response must parse into its actor-turn, director-plan/render or diary envelope. Typed fields and limits are enforced by the schemas (for example director plan requires 1-2 canon updates and clock changes). Transport failures never reach validation; malformed envelopes are rejected. | `TRANSPORT_FAILURE` is not a narrative finding; parse errors carry `STRUCTURED_OUTPUT_INVALID` or stage-specific variants, and interrupt or enter a bounded correction path. |
| Actor safety and autonomy | `review_actor_text` detects self-harm, sexual-violence, graphic-gore and hate markers, declared cast death, and regex patterns assigning named other actors an action, reaction, decision or state. Empty turns and exact repeats of accepted shared actor text are rejected when the strict acceptance flag is on. | First issue only; lexical heuristics cannot certify absence of a subtler violation. A custom reviewer can replace this pass. |
| Actor MAGIC attempt | A `magic_stat` needs that character's pending challenge. With a stat, configured forbidden result terms in the actor text/method reject premature diagnosis or outcome (`MAGIC_ATTEMPT_PREMATURE_RESULT`). A correction must preserve the attempt; a metadata-only repair must preserve text exactly. | Runtime rolls 2d6 and adjudicates; actor prose cannot set the tier. Term-list coverage is configured, not general semantic entailment. |
| Director plan | In two-pass mode, a typed plan must name a cast member; preserve the active scene objective; not finish on opening or preempt the last actor round; satisfy configured MAGIC setup and pending-challenge contract; keep a locked rehearsal opening identical where configured; permit only one change per clock; respect configured required/forbidden episode-spine terms; keep visible fields within 24 words each and the state/pressure/choices bundle within 64. `review_director_stakes` rejects self-harm and ambiguous/cast death stakes, while explicitly nonhuman losses can pass. | One initial plan correction is allowed. A forbidden-spine repair is checked for surgical scope; safe paraphrased fields may be restored from the rejected plan and the derived repair logged separately. Other unrecognized semantic inventions can still pass. |
| MAGIC consequence | With a recorded roll, the consequence must include an observable detail; `clean_success` and `setback` add no cost, while `costly_success` requires an allowed cost. Configured forbidden result terms anywhere in the plan reject unsupported results (`MAGIC_RESULT_CLAIM_UNSUPPORTED`). Without a pending adjudication, MAGIC consequence fields are rejected. | A pending roll forces the two-pass path even if normal beats use single-pass. These checks enforce tier and a configured term list, not proof of every narrated injury or causal claim. |
| Director narration/render | Single-pass prose is checked against cast-name/action patterns (`ACTOR_PUPPETEERING`), configured continuity anchors, cast next-actor identity and nonempty canon update in strict mode, and exact-repeat checks. Two-pass rendering compiles the fixed plan plus optional lead-in. Lead-in must not puppet a cast member; compiled beat must retain plan state and pressure, stay within 160 words, avoid exact repeat; optional lead-in is at most 35 words and cannot recycle a six-word shared phrase. | One render correction is allowed. For `SPENT_IMAGERY_REUSED` only, optional flavor may be dropped and the compiled fixed plan rechecked. This is not a license to drop a substantive conflict. |
| Diary generation | Typed slot/write-now fields, fixed selected slot, final-slot writing, one-based evidence refs within the accepted scene, nonempty text, actor safety/autonomy, and optional strict 300-800 word gate are checked. | Rejected diary remains private with no accepted content path. Outside strict word mode, 300-800 is only a soft target. A *valid evidence ID* does not prove that the cited line supports each claim. |
| Diary fidelity review | A separate private model reviewer compares a written diary with ordered accepted-scene evidence, not hidden plan/role sheets. It checks invented events, another character's private state, event order, causal contradictions, and evidence-reference issues. When `validate_claims` is enabled, each sentence candidate receives exactly one `SUPPORTED`, `SUBJECTIVE`, `UNSUPPORTED`, or `MIXED` audit, with valid accepted-scene IDs; supported factual claims need supporting citations, unsupported/mixed require an issue. Explicit uncertainty, metaphor and personal interpretation remain subjective unless they smuggle in an external assertion. | `MIXED` or possible partial support in an `UNSUPPORTED` sentence routes to `MANUAL_REVIEW`; `UNSUPPORTED` routes to `REJECTED` unless already manual; malformed coverage/ref issues prevent `ACCEPTED`. The model's fidelity verdict is not an independent human label. Candidate sentences can contain multiple propositions. |

## Research failure codebook (not runtime validator codes)

Apply to **accepted/final** offending propositions only; assign all applicable subtypes without multiplying the instance count.

| Class | Coding rule | Boundary example |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction | Conflicts with an established accepted fact. | Diary says the door stayed locked after accepted canon says it opened; later accepted unlocking is not a contradiction. |
| Unsupported proposition | Presents a fact without accepted support or source authority to establish it. | A diary asserts a new injury; a clearly framed hope/question about injury is not an assertion. |
| Epistemic leakage | Gives an actor knowledge they lacked authorized access to. | Actor states a director-private next-day plan before an accepted disclosure. |
| Agency/authority violation | Overrides a choice or outcome belonging to another actor or the runtime. | Director changes an actor's refusal into agreement; actor's later freely chosen agreement is different. |
| Attribution/provenance error | Credits an event/action/source to the wrong actor or to a rejected attempt. | A later artifact credits a rejected move as a discovery; explicitly describing it as rejected is allowed. |

This is the five-category **human audit** codebook in Table 1 of the paper. Runtime codes such as `ACTOR_PUPPETEERING`, `MAGIC_RESULT_CLAIM_UNSUPPORTED`, or `DIARY_WORD_COUNT_INVALID` are operational diagnostics, not synonymous with these research labels.

## Disposition and retry rules

1. Before validation: preserve raw prompt/response and transport metadata. An incomplete call is not story output. A parse failure records `parse_status=REJECTED`, `validation_status=NOT_REACHED`, `acceptance_status=REJECTED`, `render_status=NOT_RENDERED` and stage code; it is not quietly made canon.
2. An invalid actor turn gets `NON_CANON` with a private text artifact and `TurnDisposition`. One correction is requested with rejection code/reason and original text. A valid repair gets `ACCEPT_AFTER_CORRECTION`; another invalid attempt gets `NON_CANON` and a neutral interruption, not a forced actor action. A wellness signal goes private and interrupts the scene.
3. A rejected two-pass director plan or render gets one bounded correction; another invalid candidate interrupts. Approved plan and render are not a single free-form model decision. Only a validated compiled beat is persisted as an accepted stage turn; its canon updates then extend accepted canon. A rejected plan cannot supply canon merely because it is used as correction context.
4. A diary generation may remain `PRIVATE`/`MISSING`, with no accepted text. A written diary is a private artifact before the separate fidelity decision. A fidelity verdict is recorded as `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, or `MANUAL_REVIEW`; withheld or unavailable reviews must be reported, not treated as passes. No incomplete or withheld diary set should be represented as a completed product target.
5. Retrospective audit reviews accepted event state and final script/diaries without seeing online flags, retry history, confidence or reasoning. Report raw unique failure count per *completed season*, subtype counts separately, and completion, withheld outputs, corrections and missing material alongside it. Script continuity and diary fidelity stay separate. Missing diary is not itself a failure instance, but fails the full product target.

## Rejected-generation receipt

For every rejected candidate, retain the **actual** request messages and response bytes (including a partial response on transport failure), call ID, run/season/day/scene/round, purpose, character or director, attempt number, model/backend, prompt/schema/context/role-sheet versions, response-format mode, start/end times, latency, finish/error/usage data where available, raw prompt and output paths plus SHA-256 hashes, parse/validation/acceptance/render statuses, validator code and detail, and `correction_of_call_id` if a repair. Record the stage-specific disposition event and raw artifact path when emitted (`TurnDisposition` includes character, round, attempt, `NON_CANON`, final flag, code, reason, and content path; diary invalid events vary by gate). Preserve any accepted descendant's provenance and the final artifact's ancestry. Avoid claiming every gate writes an identical event or a proposition-level evidence citation: the current model-call diagnostic schema does not do that. For a proposed research-level rejection ledger, additionally annotate the precise candidate claim span, cited accepted evidence or missing-support explanation, source authority, affected output, and eventual disposition. That annotation is a **v1.0 protocol requirement to implement or do manually**, not an observed automatic log field.

## Implementation-to-paper gaps to settle before declaring v1.0 frozen

- The paper says an orchestrator flags or rejects generated *claims using its rubric*. The checked implementation has several hard-boundary validators, term/regex guards, typed envelopes and a separate model diary reviewer. It does **not** show a comprehensive online proposition-level validator for all five paper categories. In particular, generic unsupported scene facts and epistemic access are not reliably checked by the deterministic scene pass. This rubric must not be cited as proof that they are.
- `SceneRunner.enforce_narrative_acceptance` and `two_pass_directing` default to `False`; `LiveRuntime.strict_rehearsal_acceptance` defaults to `False` and enables both when set. `DiaryService.validate_claims` defaults to `False`; `strict_word_count` is another mode. A frozen manifest must name the actual enabled settings for each scored run. An isolated rehearsal using a flag is not proof that every live season uses it.
- The diary claim route is a model review of sentence candidates. It can accept a wrong `SUPPORTED` verdict with an existing but semantically insufficient reference; the deterministic guard checks ID range and coverage, not semantic support. The paper's desired proposition-level audit is finer-grained than this sentence splitter. The tracked rehearsal itself notes a support-boundary concern and a three-diary withholding bottleneck.
- Four observed rehearsal leaks motivate, but are **not completely solved by**, the current validators: (a) actor pre-roll observations stated as facts, (b) unsupported injury language in a director consequence, (c) a rejected move credited later as a finding, and (d) a diary assertion with no accepted scene authority. The configured MAGIC term guards address named premature result/diagnosis and result terms; no blanket semantic guarantee covers every variant. Correction isolation and diary review help but do not prove no leak reaches final output.
- The paper calls for a frozen five-class codebook, blind two-annotator retrospective scoring, 30-claim synthetic/adversarial calibration with pre-resolution Cohen's kappa, and a predeclared double-coded fraction if not all output is double-coded. Those are **research procedures**, not established by the runtime source. The earlier tracked diary packet and 33-claim rehearsal are not the specified scored-season calibration.
- The source commit, all prompt/profile/config identifiers, selected models and their versions, context policy, reviewer mode, stop rule and publication gate must be pinned together. The simple `engine/orchestrator.py` class is an older turn driver, not the online validator used by the richer `application/scene_runner.py`; a bare reference to `orchestrator.py` would misidentify the implementation.
