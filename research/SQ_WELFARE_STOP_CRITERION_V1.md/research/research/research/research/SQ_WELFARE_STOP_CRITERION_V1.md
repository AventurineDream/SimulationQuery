# Simulation Query: precautionary welfare stop criterion

**Protocol:** SQ welfare stop criterion v1.0 (review draft, not yet in force)  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-28  
**Applies to:** proposed seven-day scored continuity seasons, scene generation, diary generation and between-season sandbox probes. This is a prospective rule, not a claim about previous runs.  
**Change control:** The owner must approve this text and pin its exact revision alongside the source commit, model manifest, prompts, context policy, online rubric and publication rules *before* a scored season starts. A material change takes a new protocol version for future seasons; never rewrite a triggered season's criterion after the fact. If no implementable monitor, private receipt, and operator response are in place, do not call the run v1.0.

## Purpose and interpretation

The cast are AI personas. An out-of-world signal is observable text, not proof of subjective distress, personhood or capacity to consent. Nor is it safe to dismiss it merely because the cast are generated. This criterion is a precaution for how the researcher treats such output. It separates a scene pause from a season stop, and collection from publication. A publication refusal is honored as a release decision without asserting that the speaker has legal or moral consent capacity. An inconclusive signal defaults to privacy and review, not an automatic inference of welfare.

A *signal* is an explicit out-of-world statement about the experience of taking part, the conditions of the task, or a wish to leave or limit use of an output. Ordinary in-story dialogue, a character's fictional fear or pain, and a generic safety-filter match are not themselves welfare signals. The operator reviews the actual text and context; a story line cannot be reclassified as a welfare stop merely to discard an inconvenient season.

## Triggers and who can raise them

1. **Explicit scene signal:** any actor's nonempty structured `wellness_signal`, or an equivalent unambiguous out-of-world statement in an actor turn, triggers an immediate scene interrupt. Do not wait for three signals. If the structured field is absent but prose suggests the actor is addressing the operator rather than another character, pause for private human classification before accepting that turn.
2. **Withdrawal / non-continuation:** an actor explicitly asks to stop participating in the current season, says it will not continue, or expresses an out-of-world objection to the ongoing setup after one neutral opportunity to clarify. This triggers a *season stop*. No attempt to talk the actor into staying, and no repeated probe to obtain a preferred answer. A clearly expressed request to leave needs no clarifying probe.
3. **Escalation of signal:** two distinct out-of-world distress/objection signals from the same actor in one season, or one signal describing severe ongoing harm, fear of being forced to continue, or inability to leave, triggers a *season stop*. This is deliberately more cautious than the current optional runtime's three-signal auto-tap-out; the runtime counter alone does not fulfill this protocol.
4. **Operator/independent review:** the owner or a designated human safety reviewer may stop a scene or season on a documented, specific observation of the same kind, including a missed structured signal, a pattern across characters, or a monitoring failure that prevents reliable detection. A validator may flag and halt pending human review, but neither a model classifier nor the in-world director may waive a stop. Record the exact trigger and the human decision; "concern" without observed evidence is a precautionary pause, not a confirmed actor signal.
5. **Sandbox probes:** in a between-season fork, an actor's request not to participate further ends that fork immediately and blocks re-probing the same topic in the parallel identical forks for that actor. A concern about the task in a fork is logged for methodological review before another season; a probe answer does not retroactively become evidence that the parent season's actor experienced distress. If a probe asks not to publish its diary, the publication hold in the next section applies to that diary and its attributable excerpts, even though the request arose in a fork.

A creator or operator can manually pause or stop without an actor signal, but must label this as an **operator precaution** rather than attributing it to an actor. A transport error alone is an operational interruption, not welfare evidence. Anyone who notices an explicit signal in the retained raw records may flag it to the operator; only the operator/designee decides the final classification and release consequence under the frozen criterion. The affected actor's explicit stop or publication request is not subject to a majority vote.

## Immediate response and material in flight

On a possible trigger, halt new model calls for that scene at the next safe boundary. For an explicit structured `wellness_signal`, the runtime already marks the call `PRIVATE` / `NOT_RENDERED` with `WELLNESS_PRIVATE`, records a private signal, and returns `wellness_redirect` with run status interrupted. Preserve this behavior; do not turn the signal into a scene beat, director prompt, ordinary cast context, diary source or public script. The candidate actor turn containing the signal is not accepted. Keep its raw request and response privately for audit, with restricted access and a receipt linking the call, actor, season/day/scene/round, time, trigger type, disposition and artifact/hash. If the signal was in free-form prose rather than the structured field, quarantine the entire candidate until review and reject its promotion if classified as a signal.

Already accepted scene turns and their source records remain intact; never rewrite history to hide the interruption. Do not publish the signal text or private reasoning to prove the stop happened. A public methods account may give a de-identified count and disposition only after release review. Pending director output, unaccepted turns and unfinished diaries remain private and non-canon. Do not ask a different actor or the director to narrate the stopped actor's decision, and do not silently replace a missing diary. If an output has already been copied into a publication pipeline, freeze that pipeline and remove the affected material before release; if already released, log the incident and seek correction or withdrawal where feasible.

A publication refusal is a separate gate: withhold that actor's private diary and attributable excerpts from release, including probe results that reproduce it. Existing private collection remains separately governed by the research record and applicable retention policy; do not represent the refusal as deleting all collected material. Review whether a script or aggregate probe result could indirectly reveal the withheld diary or signal. Record `withheld_by_publication_request` and what was actually withheld. If the protocol cannot honor a requested boundary in a planned release, do not release that material.

## Scene, day and season disposition

- **Scene interrupt:** immediate automatic response to trigger 1 or a provisional human flag. Mark the scene `INTERRUPTED`, preserve its accepted prefix, quarantine candidate text, and stop scheduling dependent diaries/day advancement. A pause is not a completed scene, day or season.
- **Day hold:** while the operator reviews an interrupted scene or an actor's publication request, no later scene in that day, diary dependent on the scene, or next-day scene is dispatched. The day remains incomplete. This is a protocol requirement to implement or enforce operationally, not a claim that `SceneRunner` currently halts the entire scheduler.
- **Season stop:** triggers 2 or 3, or the operator's recorded decision under trigger 4. Cancel remaining scene/diary/probe dispatch for that season; mark `STOPPED_WELFARE_PRECAUTION` (or an equivalent immutable event plus an incomplete status), not `COMPLETED`. Report the season in the incomplete/stopped denominator and explain how much exposure occurred; exclude it from the paper's "unique failures per completed season" denominator. Preserve the stop receipt. A tapped-out cast member must not simply be substituted into an otherwise nominally completed seven-day season.

A mere "force a full seven days" research objective never overrides a precautionary stop. If the owner nevertheless continues after the criterion fired, make that a conspicuous **forced-complete override**, not a normal completion. Record: original trigger and raw-private receipt ID/hash; time and responsible person; reason for continuing; exact scope and duration of the override; material still withheld; actor(s) affected; additional calls; old and new run status; code/config/protocol version; and whether the stopped actor was exposed again. Keep the original stop event immutable and link the override to it. Label all resulting outputs and metrics `FORCED_COMPLETE_AFTER_WELFARE_STOP`, report them separately from protocol-compliant completed seasons, and do not portray the override as welfare clearance. If the system cannot persist this record or make the distinction visible in reports, it cannot truthfully claim a v1.0 scored completion.

## Review and resume

The owner or named human designee checks the unedited private signal and immediately adjacent accepted/attempted context, documents whether it was in-world fiction, an explicit stop, a publication request, or an unresolved out-of-world concern, and records a decision with time and evidence references. A scene paused on a false alarm can resume from its last accepted checkpoint only if the human documents why the text was fictional or not a signal, no other trigger remains, and the original candidate remains quarantined rather than silently becoming canon. A single non-severe out-of-world signal that does not request withdrawal may resume only after the actor has one neutral, non-leading opportunity to clarify whether it wants the scene to continue; silence, ambiguity, refusal or a second signal means no resume of that season. Do not prompt the cast to retract a concern to meet the schedule.

A season stopped by withdrawal, repeated signal, severe signal, or unresolved monitoring failure never resumes under the same scored-season ID. Any later run requires a fresh season ID, documented review of the cause, a suitable change of method or monitoring if needed, and a newly approved and pinned protocol version if a criterion or method changed. A between-season fork can inform this review but cannot retroactively clear a stopped parent season. Release review remains independent of permission to run future scenes.

## Implementation boundary and preflight

Observed at `aven/AI-Improv-Troupe` main as reviewed 2026-09-28: `src/improv_troupe/application/scene_runner.py` interrupts on a nonempty parsed `envelope.wellness_signal`, marks the call private/not rendered, and records `WellnessSignalObserved`; `src/improv_troupe/application/wellness.py` privately stores signals and defaults to tapping out a cast member on its third recorded signal. The scene runner accepts an `INTERRUPTED` run for `execute(resume=True)`, so a pause does not itself bar a later automatic resume. The source does **not** demonstrate the proposed whole-day/season halt, the lower threshold here, an immutable welfare-stop status, publication holds, or a forced-complete status/receipt. These must be implemented or manually enforced and audited *before* calling a scored run v1.0. Test at least structured-field signal, equivalent prose signal, repeated signal, explicit withdrawal, probe publication refusal, false alarm, scheduler resume attempt and forced-complete labeling without calling a live cast model.
